# Meta creative health

Read [the analysis principles](../references/ads-analysis-principles.md),
[response pattern](../references/workflow-response-pattern.md), and
[Meta reference](../manage-ads/references/meta-ads.md).

Resolve the brand and Meta connection. Use complete comparable dates supplied by
the user; otherwise choose a recent period and an equal preceding period in the
account timezone. Inspect campaign objective, delivery and creative inventory
before interpreting performance.

Start with the account fatigue summary, ad-set diversity and ad-level delivery.
Rank candidates by spend and objective impact, then inspect creative details and
matched asset or video evidence for at most five ads that can materially change
the verdict. Expand only when the user asks or the first pass cannot explain a
material result. Use Meta performance groups and asset breakdowns that match the
question: delivery and links for spend/click outcomes; video for hook, watch and
completion signals; rankings for ad diagnostics; creative fatigue and diversity
where Meta returns them; image, video, copy, format and destination asset
breakdowns for matched-asset comparisons. Follow the Meta reference's level and
pagination rules, and do not treat an unavailable preset as zero performance.

Prefer each ad's own earlier complete performance as its baseline. Read CTR,
CPM, frequency and objective-aligned cost together. Frequency growth or CTR
decline alone is a warning, not proof of fatigue. Check recent edits, learning
state, delivery mix and volume before naming a cause. Compare videos at the
earliest supported viewing milestone; never turn aggregate playback into an
individual viewer journey.

Quietly determine whether the data supports a trend, a point-in-time health read,
or only inventory. Rank attention by business impact—such as spend flowing
through a deteriorating ad—not by the largest percentage movement on tiny volume.

Return a concise verdict and, when supported:

- creatives to keep running and why;
- a refresh queue with the evidence and spend at risk;
- the earliest video or creative signal that weakened;
- a bounded next test or production brief based on patterns in proven winners.

Do not claim a creative is tired from a generic frequency or CTR benchmark. This
analysis changes nothing: offer an exact prepared pause, a new paused photo ad or a
shareable creative brief. Apply a pause or new ad only through the
[manage-ads workflow](../manage-ads/SKILL.md) after approval. Replacing an existing
ad's creative is unavailable. Never imply a change was applied.

Stay in the merchant's role; do not ask them to role-play an agency or creative
profession. When the analysis produces a useful next step, always end with up to
three numbered actions Mighty can start now, such as preparing a refresh brief,
examining one named creative more deeply or creating a shareable summary. If the
merchant selects a number, begin that work instead of repeating the options.
